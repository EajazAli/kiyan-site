Right Reach

The question

Are you present in the right store universe and segments, where shoppers buy?

Know every outlet that matters, then cover the ones that pay.

A verified store universe replaces estimated counts and blind spots, so field effort is aimed at the outlets that carry the category.

Key solutions inside this pillar

Retail census & store universe

  • Every outlet scanned and mapped to GIS coordinates: 5M+ entities across three markets.
  • Stores auto-graded Gold, Silver or Bronze from the premium brands on their shelves.
ImpactA verified store universe replaces estimated counts and blind spots.

Channel structure & coverage design

  • Outlets classified into the KSA channel framework: modern trade, self service, traditional trade, wholesale and HORECA.

ImpactField effort aimed at the outlets that carry the category.

A store is more than its name, address and sales history.

A retail universe is not a store list or a map of pins. It is a verified, decision-ready view of every relevant outlet: where it is, what type of store it is, what happens inside it, what surrounds it and what commercial potential it represents.

LayerWhat Kiyan understandsWhy it matters
Outside the storeLocation, catchment, traffic, surrounding activity and competitionReveals opportunity and context
The store itselfChannel, format, operating profile, footprint, storage and receivingDefines how the store can be served
Inside the storeCategory presence, assortment baseline, shelf reality, cooling and displayShows where performance can be won

Most census exercises provide a directory. Kiyan provides the intelligence needed to act.

Find the leakage before redesigning the route.

A route can look busy while still being commercially inefficient. Kiyan examines the relationship between route activity, store coverage, product availability and cost-to-serve.

01

Coverage leakage

Viable stores in the service area that are not being reached.

02

Route leakage

Overlapping, backtracking or unnecessarily long routes.

03

Assortment leakage

Vehicle load mismatched to store needs.

04

Frequency leakage

Stores over-visited while valuable stores are under-served.

05

Capacity leakage

Vehicle or salesperson capacity used unproductively.

06

Commercial leakage

Route effort not aligned with store potential or return.

Design the route around the opportunity, not the other way around.

Kiyan does not optimise distance alone. A shorter route is not better if it misses high-potential outlets, carries the wrong assortment, creates excess frequency or delivers low-value drops.

  • Covers more relevant stores with available assets.

  • Increases productive calls and selling time.
  • Creates room for sales uplift without adding resources.

Right Range

The question

Is the 80/20 still working, or is it quietly costing you?

A productive assortment that protects shopper needs.

It removes duplication, congestion and inventory fragmentation without losing the shopper.

Range architecture80/20 analysisDelisting simulationCannibalisation

Key solutions inside this pillar

Range architecture

  • Range built from demand spaces: must-stock versus nice-to-have, need-state coverage, walk-out and switching risk.
ImpactThe retailer protects shopper needs; the manufacturer defends must-stock lines.

Assortment productivity & listing/delisting simulation

  • Pareto tail analysis, space-to-sales and rate of sale by store cluster.
  • Simulation of what walks in or out, what switches and what is lost.
ImpactThe retailer optimises inventory; the manufacturer improves category relevance.

Cannibalisation & innovation fit testing

  • Choice-based conjoint (CBC) for true substitution and source of volume.
  • Incremental versus transferable demand per SKU.
ImpactThe retailer lists incremental inventory; the manufacturer lists innovation to win.

04 / Pillars

Right Store

The question

Does one national planogram really fit every store cluster?

Cluster, map and size the opportunity store by store.

Cluster-specific range, space and service models replace one national planogram.

Store clusteringSegmentationShopper flowAdjacency

Key solutions inside this pillar

Store clustering & segmentation

  • Stores clustered on shopper profile, mission mix and demand-space skew.
  • Cluster-specific range, space and service models instead of one national planogram.
ImpactHigher rate of sale from cluster-fit range and store-specific planogram execution.

Category adjacency & macro space planning

  • In-store flow study mapping paths, decisions and dwell time by zone.
  • Hot and cold zones identified to improve category rate of sale.
ImpactCategory penetration, basket size, visibility and experience improved.

A segmentation model that tells every team what to do differently.

The value of segmentation is not knowing which stores belong together. It is knowing what to do differently with each group.

What you receive
  • Store segmentation model. Classification by performance, potential, role and investment.
  • Segment definitions & criteria. The variables, thresholds and logic behind each segment.
  • Store-level segment database. Every outlet linked to its segment and attributes.
  • Segment-specific playbooks. Assortment, MSL, planogram, coverage and activation.
  • Prioritised opportunity view. Stores where investment can unlock more potential.
  • Measurement framework. Segment-specific KPIs for availability and sales.
What it changes commercially
  • Prioritises high-potential stores rather than treating all outlets equally.
  • Identifies stores where current sales understate real opportunity.
  • Matches assortment and MSL to each store's role and potential.
  • Allocates field-force time and visit frequency more efficiently.
  • Improves the return from assets, promotions and activation.
  • Strengthens the foundation for route-to-market, planogram and activation.

Right Space

The question

Is the share of shelf in line with the share of sales and profitability?

Balance four needs before recommending space.

The Mixed Demand Space model weighs commercial, shopper, operational and strategic needs. Planograms are built for every fixture type, from gondolas, chillers and freezers to check-out, through to a full 3D store layout.

Space to salesGMROSMacro & micro spaceStore-specific planograms

Key solutions inside this pillar

Space-to-sales & profitability analysis

  • Share of shelf versus share of sales, margin and GMROS.
  • Over-spaced and under-spaced categories identified, with the profit at stake.
ImpactHigher return per square metre and fact-based shelf-share allocation.

Macro & micro space planning

  • Macro: category space allocation, floor plan and adjacency by store format.
  • Space elasticity modelled before any change is committed.
ImpactBetter traffic flow, basket size and cross-category pull.

Store-specific planograms

  • Cluster and store-level planograms sized to real demand and fixture reality.
  • Compliance and on-shelf availability tracked so executed space matches the plan.
ImpactShopper-centric plans implemented store by store, with better space and inventory efficiency.

One category. Different stores. Smarter shelf decisions.

A planogram must reflect the store’s segment, role and physical reality. A strong planogram is standardised in logic, but adapted to reality.

Store requirementPlanogram response
High-potential, high-volume storeBroader assortment, more facings, stronger visibility, fuller category block
Growth-opportunity storePrioritised MSL, clear hero SKUs, space to support distribution growth
Limited-space storeEssential assortment, compact blocking, disciplined space allocation
Efficient-service storeProductive core range with simple, easy-to-maintain execution standards

Right Execution

The question

Is your on-shelf availability where it needs to be?

On-shelf availability is decided at the shelf. We close the loop every week.

Image recognition gives a consistent, objective baseline instead of manual spot checks. Execution stays with your team; we track whether each task closed.

AI shelf auditOSA & share of shelfComplianceGap to task
96–98%audit benchmark
2 minper audit
−60%audit cost versus manual audit

Key solutions inside this pillar

AI image-recognition shelf audit

  • Every store, every week: field-force capture at two minutes per audit.
  • Every SKU photographed and every shelf position recorded.
ImpactA consistent, objective baseline instead of manual spot checks.

OSA, share of shelf & planogram compliance

  • On-shelf availability, share of shelf and compliance from the same capture.
  • Gaps surfaced by SKU, shelf position and store, before they cost margin.
ImpactShare of shelf tracked against share of sales to expose execution drift.

Gap-to-task closure & verification

  • Each gap becomes a task ticket with a specific SKU, position and resolve window.
  • Rebuilt shelves are re-captured the same week.
ImpactOnly closed tasks count towards the KPI.

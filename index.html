The execution gap

Strategy is rarely the problem. The shelf is where it leaks.

Brand owners already hold POS data, syndicated reports, supplier decks and category reviews. Strategy and execution live in different silos, so insight rarely becomes a price tag, a planogram or a store action. OSA, share and margin are confirmed in-store, not in the slide deck.

  • BasketsSmaller baskets, lost sales

The six pillars

From the right stores to the right shelf, in order.

Each pillar answers one of the six questions and hands its output to the next. An engagement can start at any pillar.

01

Right Reach

Know every outlet that matters, then cover the ones that pay.

  • Retail census and store universe. Every outlet scanned and mapped to GIS coordinates, graded Gold, Silver or Bronze from the brands on its shelves.
  • Channel structure and coverage. Outlets classified into modern trade, self-service, traditional trade, wholesale and HORECA; call plans sized by outlet value, density and city tier.
  • Store intelligence. Outside the store (catchment, traffic, competition), the store itself (format, footprint, receiving) and inside it (category presence, shelf, chiller, display).

ImpactA verified store universe replaces estimated counts and blind spots. Field effort goes to the outlets that carry the category.

5M+outlets scanned across three markets
230+KSA cities covered
18+years of census fieldwork
02

Right Category

Give every category a role, then plan it jointly with the retailer.

  • Category captaincy architecture. Category roles built from shopper research, trip triggers, basket and loyalty data; a supplier-neutral range and space blueprint; white-space and innovation.
  • Joint business planning. End-to-end project management from insight to planning to in-store execution, with a category calendar and forecast.

ImpactA neutral-party category growth plan that moves from slides to shelf.

03

Right Range

A productive assortment protects shopper needs and removes duplication, congestion and fragmented inventory.

  • Range architecture. Must-stock versus nice-to-have, need-state coverage, walk-out and switching risk, built from demand spaces.
  • Listing and delisting simulation. Pareto tail, space-to-sales and rate of sale by store cluster: what walks in, what switches, what is genuinely lost.
  • Cannibalisation and innovation fit. Choice-based conjoint for true substitution and source of volume; incremental versus transferable demand per SKU.

ImpactThe retailer protects shopper needs and inventory; the manufacturer defends must-stock lines and lists innovation that wins.

80/20: a few lines carry the category

04

Right Store

Cluster, map and size the opportunity store by store.

  • Store clustering and segmentation. Clusters by shopper profile, mission mix and demand-space skew, with cluster-specific range, space and service models instead of one national planogram.
  • Category adjacency and macro space. In-store flow studies map paths, decisions and dwell time by zone, so hot and dead zones are known before the category is placed.

ImpactCluster-fit range lifts rate of sale; adjacency improves penetration, basket size and visibility.

05

Right Space

Shelf space is allocated on evidence, not on who shouts loudest.

  • Space-to-sales and profitability. Share of shelf is compared with share of sales, margin and return per metre, so over-spaced and under-spaced categories are visible, with the profit at stake.
  • Macro and micro space planning. Category allocation, floor plan and adjacency by store format, with space elasticity modelled before anything is moved.
  • Store-specific planograms. One for every fixture type, from gondolas and chillers to check-out, with weekly compliance and availability tracking so the executed shelf matches the plan.

ImpactHigher return per square metre and a shelf share the whole table can defend with facts.

06

Right Execution

On-shelf availability is decided at the shelf. We close the loop every week.

  • AI image-recognition shelf audit. Every store, every week, captured by the field force in about two minutes; every SKU photographed and every shelf position recorded.
  • OSA, share of shelf and compliance. All three from the same capture, with gaps surfaced by SKU, position and store before they cost margin.
  • Gap-to-task closure. Each gap becomes a ticket with a resolve window. Your team executes; rebuilt shelves are re-captured the same week and only closed tasks count.

ImpactAn objective weekly baseline instead of manual spot checks, and execution drift caught before it reaches the P&L.
